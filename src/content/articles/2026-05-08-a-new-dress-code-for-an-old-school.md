Being the self-proclaimed “oldest continually operated” boarding school in the United States comes with a certain level of prestige and pride. Walking on the same ground that thousands of students and teachers have trodden before carries weight. However, if those same alumni and faculty could see what the school looks like now, they’d be taken aback. The first thing they would notice when they returned is the dress code.

Should Govs go back to a stricter dress code? From the beginning, Governor Dummer Academy has been an institution based on tradition. When a visitor steps on campus, they see the Red School House, the Milestone, the Mansion House–so many old and distinguished sites. Then they turn and see ripped jeans, leggings, sweatshirts, and pajama pants. The contrast can be jarring.

During Accepted Students Day, we were asked to “dress up”; specifically, no sweatpants. Some in the community were critical because they thought it presented a false sense of what the Govs experience would be to the prospective students. But perhaps this is how we should look every day so that we can honor the history and beauty of the campus. Looking presentable on a day like Accepted Students Day is crucial because it sends a strong, positive message to the new kids and parents. The way we present ourselves contributes to how others perceive not just individual students, but the entire community. But it can also affect how we see ourselves and our place in the long tradition of GDA.

We sent out a survey asking if Govs should have a stricter dress code or not, and 160 people responded. Overall, 60% said we should keep the current dress code, and 40% wanted something more formal. Of the faculty that responded, 75% of the responses were in favor of a stricter dress code. This survey does not conclusively suggest that we should reinstate a stronger dress code, but it does hint at a desire to consider some changes to the rules we have now.

The dress code has changed many times throughout the long history of the Governor’s Academy. In the past 25 years, however, it has changed more drastically than the 250 years before. The school has replaced tradition with comfort, and we are not better for it. Part of the appeal of prep schools is that they are preppy. When you dress well, you feel good, and that translates to increased confidence inside and outside of the classroom. We should strive to be a school that is better dressed than the eighth graders coming for tours.

**Timeline**

**Up through 1971**

–All male school.

–Coats and ties required.

–Short haircuts mandatory.

–Formal dress for sit-down dinners.

**Coeducation through 2015**

–For girls: dresses or collared shirts with skirts or pants.

–Shirts could not show shoulders.

–For boys: slacks (no jeans) with collared shirt and belt.

–No hats allowed inside buildings.

–Struggle with enforcement.

**Current Day**

–Gender Neutral dress code.

–”Must look presentable,” but few other guidelines.

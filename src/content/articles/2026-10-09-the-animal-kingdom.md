Govs campus is home to some unusual pets, including Mrs. McLain’s pig Haru, and Mrs. Borgatti’s litter of puppies. Sixteen years ago, an unusual request to babysit a pig became a large addition to Mr. and Mrs. McLain’s family. Mrs. Borgatti helps to breed dogs and often has a bunch of puppies running around. Both the pig and puppies provide good stories and bring a sense of life to campus.

The special name of Mrs. McLain’s pig comes from, "Haru, the great white ninja of whom the prophecies speak of," a quote from the 1997 martial arts comedy film *Beverly Hills Ninja.* When her daughter was a senior at Bowdoin, she asked her parents to babysit the pig during March break. The pig came to Byfield for two weeks and was then sent back to college. Not long after, he was back at Govs because, “it's really hard to keep an illegal pig in the dorms at a college.”

Haru has a rigorous schedule of sleeping and belly rubs. He is old now so he is not as active, but Mrs. McLain said, “when he was little he would wander across the turf looking for apples under the wild trees.” He also enjoys the fireplace. A fun fact about pigs is that they don't sweat, contradicting the common misconception that they smell. According to Mrs. McLain, they are very clean animals.

It’s much more common to find someone with a dog or two instead of a pig, but Mrs. Borgatti goes beyond the expected; instead of one or two dogs, she has raised nine puppies. Mrs. Borgatti works with a breeder and takes care of litters of puppies that will grow up to be in working homes in search and rescue, medical alert, therapy, and hunting careers. She said “It’s a good thing they are cute, because they are high maintenance.” She needs to sleep near the pups for the first couple weeks so they don't wander off or get cold during the night. Puppies can't see or hear for the first 14 days, and she also has to make sure they get enough calcium while they are young. As they grow older, the puppies need to be fed every 2-3 hours to ensure their growth, making it hard for Mrs. Borgatti to be gone for too long.

Mrs. Borgatti shared a story of a pup who kept roaming freely around the house and mysteriously escaped her “gated community” frequently. She was confused how she got free while the others were still in the gated area. Mrs. Borgatti happened to come home right when the puppy was scaling the fence to escape while all the other puppies just watched.

These pets provide a sense of home to the school community. Though the boarders have to leave their pets to come to Govs, they can still have connections to animals on campus.

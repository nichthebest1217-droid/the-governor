Is the three-activity rule outdated at Govs? Many of us ask that question because we have been forced to do a sport or activity we didn’t want in the first place. However, it has also allowed students to try new activities and engage in life on campus.

At the academy, all students must participate in one sport or activity each season. For example, I play football in the fall, strength and conditioning during the winter, then lacrosse in the spring. However, freshmen and sophomores must play a sport or participate in the arts and cannot do activities like strength/conditioning until they are upperclassmen.

I asked Mrs. Barcomb, the athletic director at Govs, about the origin and intentions of the rule. She explained that, as a boarding school, the requirement was created to keep students engaged and on campus.

When this rule was introduced, out-of-school commitments weren’t as common, especially before COVID: “It \[COVID\] definitely changed the dynamic of athletes regularly doing three sports, as it was much more common to see that, unlike today,” she said.

I also asked Mrs. Barcomb about whether she would change this rule. She said, “With the right support and figuring out what to do with kids while they have a season off, this could be a possibility in the future for maybe seniors to only have to do two seasons.” Her biggest concern was for underclassmen boarding students having nothing to do once the school day concluded. She said it “defeats the purpose of the structure that boarding schools are supposed to have.”

Boarding student Porter LaLime ‘27, a football and baseball player, said, “I understand the idea of it, but most people would agree that they want a season off.” Porter felt that giving students more options would be useful. He especially wanted “to make an independent activity more of an availability here.”

At my old school, you were allowed to participate in an independent activity, where you signed off on your own activity and were excused from afternoon programs. This allowed students to pursue an out-of-school commitment like a club sport or another activity not affiliated with their school.

Porter agreed with this approach, saying, “In the past I’d use the winter to train for baseball, but with the rule, it took my time away to train in the off-season.”

The three-activity rule at Govs is a hotly debated topic as it serves benefits but also has downsides. It works because it keeps students engaged on campus, busy, and able to try new activities. On the flip side, it can take away an important off-season for athletes or force students into activities they don’t want.

How long can the school hang on to this policy in the world of club sports and outside commitments?

Junior year at Govs is like the first day of learning how to swim – an inevitable, painful, but also rewarding experience. Thesis paper is coming, harder classes are waiting, and college application is looming, making junior year indeed a challenging year. Ms. Allen, from the learning center, has some useful tips to conquer this difficult year.

One thing that Ms. Allen emphasized was to find “hidden time” – times we often wasted scrolling or hanging out with friends. Although staying with friends is good for mental health, the correct approach is to “treat it as a reward rather than a way to procrastinate.” “When things start piling up,” Ms. Allen mentioned, “we all know how stressed we get.”

Another thing Ms. Allen brought up was to learn from failure. “We never learn how to deal with the times when we are disappointed in ourselves without learning from some minor failures.” She gave the instance that if students did badly on a test, it could be an opportunity to learn better test-taking skills. Be resilient; don’t let failure compound itself to make a bigger failure.

Finally, Ms. Allen suggests prioritizing a free block in junior’s schedules. A free block has two benefits: it gives you more time to catch up on work, and it helps you to exercise your time management skills. In college, students will have fewer class times and more free time. Having a free block familiarizes you with college life earlier, and you will become better at using your free time wisely.

In addition, here are some of my personal perspectives that might be helpful:

1\. Perfectionism is often detrimental. Don’t set too many goals and try to achieve every one of them. Know your priorities, which might be your grades or sport, and put most of the effort into that.

2\. Having a good mood boosts your productivity. Getting some sunlight, staying hydrated, and eating enough usually makes the mood better. Most importantly, don’t be afraid to talk to your friends, parents, and the counselors at the health center when you are having an especially bad week.

3\. Be honest about how much sleep you need. Only a few people can sustain themselves with very little sleep. Sacrificing sleep often compromises efficiency, ending up worsening the results compared to getting enough sleep. Try not to drink coffee after 3 PM. Caffeine could stay in your body and hurt your sleep quality.

Getting through junior year not only requires better time management but also better mood, lifestyle, and mindset management. Believe me: the start is always the hardest. The further you progress into the year, the better you get. Good luck to all of you!

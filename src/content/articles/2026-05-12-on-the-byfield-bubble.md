Those of us interested in politics around campus are well aware of the so-called “Byfield Bubble,”our school’s seeming isolation from national events. Even in the Polite Political Discussions (PPD) the year before last, much of our discussion revolved around topics only tangentially related to national politics. The question is, has Govs always been like this? Should we change?

By talking to Ms. Slater, History teacher and School Archivist, I was able to dig deeper into our school’s political history. Through this, I learned that Govs has always toed the line of neutrality. There have been a few exceptions to our school’s political isolation, though only in times of national crisis like the two World Wars. During both of those, less athletic afternoon programs were cancelled so that students could become more physically fit and ready to serve their country. However, this has never been done since then, and given social norms and widespread antiwar beliefs in our modern era, it seems this will never happen again. So, the question remains: Should we “pop” the Byfield bubble?

As national politics become more divisive, it becomes harder to openly discuss them with friends for fear of getting pushed away due to differing opinions. Our PPD meetings in the fall and winter of 2024 didn’t always relate directly to national politics, but they gave us licence to discuss important issues. It was for that reason I was happy to see another PPD meeting early this school year on freedom of speech. From what I saw, it seemed like mostly everyone agreed on that issue. However, we haven’t had another meeting since then, even though political strife has continued outside of our bubble.

In a midterm year like this one, especially with current political issues, it would be productive to bring back PPD. Besides allowing students to talk about politics, this will encourage them to keep involved in and educated about elections, whether local, statewide, or national. After all, if a school’s purpose is to prepare its students for life as adults, it is important that students are taught the importance of fulfilling their civic duty of voting. Only about 45% of the world’s population has that opportunity, so we need to make it count!

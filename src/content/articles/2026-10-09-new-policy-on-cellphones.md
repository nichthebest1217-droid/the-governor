After a brutal math class where it seems as though the teacher is speaking another language and a cold science class out in the marsh by Alfond, all students want to do is relax in the dining hall with their friends and check their phones for texts, emails, fantasy football scores, or social media. However, students must remind themselves that phones can't be visible in the dining hall. Do cell phones make our generation more “brainrotted” and worse at creating conversations? Or, can we use them appropriately to relax while still connecting with the community?

This school year, students are prohibited from having their phone visible in the dining hall from 11 am to 1 pm. Mr. Delay said that "Having no phones allows for social interaction and more personal conversation.” While there's no doubt that this is true and lunch is an amazing opportunity for making a friend, so much of what we do in this generation is on our phones and it doesn't stop people from making conversation or meeting a new classmate. Connecting with others can involve taking a phone out to get someone's snapchat or instagram. Ty Harrington ‘29 argues that “Simply talking about stuff on your phone could be a conversation starter.”

The idea of no phones during this time isn't an unbearable burden to students, but they would still like to check it for quick reassurance so that they aren't missing out on what's going on in the world or with their friends and family. We're nervous of our phones getting taken away when we simply want to check the time. Cole Travaglini ‘29 says “What if I need to see if a teacher has changed a meeting time?”

Cole Cournoyer ‘28 argues that phones don't need to be fully allowed but a simple glance, text, email, or quick check should be allowed. “But sitting there scrolling, not interacting with people should not be allowed.”

My simple, and easy solution is that phones should only be used for a quick check or a glance for the time while we are eating lunch. Perhaps teachers could ease up slightly on the taking of cell phones in the dining hall. But students who are sitting there and not interacting with peers should be reminded to put it away. Let's embrace what's a part of our generation, while also being present in our beautiful community.

If you’ve ever taken a math class in the basement of the math and science building, you’ve probably seen a middle-aged man wearing a GDA shirt and watching Fortnite Streaming. That man is Mr. Hunt, a math teacher and the wrestling coach at the Governor’s Academy. He has recently announced that he is stepping away from his legendary coaching career at Govs.

He started coaching wrestling in 2003 and became the head coach in 2012. In his decades serving the academy, Mr. Hunt coached 67 ISL All-League wrestlers, 24 All-NEPSAC wrestlers, and 5 All-Americans, and guided numerous athletes into collegiate wrestling programs.

The beginning of Mr. Hunt’s wrestling program wasn’t just sunshine and rainbows, though. He went winless in his first and second years, and only won one match in his third year. His inspiration to become a high school wrestling coach came from his first wrestling coach, who “never gave up on him in the beginning of his career,” and from his desire to bring that belief to the next generation of wrestlers.

Mr. Hunt was not an easy coach, though. He pushed the team to the best of our abilities in the conditioning and live sessions, because he “wants his wrestlers to learn how to face hardship in their life.” I remember being drained from sprints one day and hearing him say, “You feel tired now, but in ten minutes you will feel better. Life is hard.”

As a wrestler on the team, one of my most memorable moments happened this year in a trio meet with BB&N and Middlesex. During my second match with Middlesex, I faced a kid who was stronger than me. When the first period ended, I was exhausted and down 3 – 0. Mr. Hunt yelled at me, “Go top! Think of a breakdown to a pin.” As the whistle blew, I drove my opponent into the mat and pinned him. As I walked back to our bench, Mr. Hunt tapped my shoulder and said, “It’s tiring, right? But doesn’t it feel good? I know that kid is bigger than you, but he doesn’t have what you have.” I don’t remember the rest of the day, but his words stuck with me ever since.

Tim Duncan, the basketball Hall of Famer once said, “you know it’s time to go when the young guys are doing things you used to do.” That was Mr. Hunt’s explanation for stepping down as the head coach. He told me it is one of his responsibilities to know when to pass the torch to the next guy, and he felt “his time has come.” Thank you, Mr. Hunt, for your years of dedicated hard work for the Governor’s wrestling program.

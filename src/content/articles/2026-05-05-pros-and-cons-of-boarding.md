As a boarding student who lives only 30 minutes away, I used to always consider appealing to switch to day students status. Why move my life somewhere else if I can just commute every day? After four years of sticking it out and serving as a dorm proctor, I can honestly say that I made the right decision. My experience has been mostly positive, though there are positives to both the boarding and day student experiences.

One difference between boarders and day students is the access to resources on campus. When you are boarding, your life revolves around campus. It is easy to access the library throughout the day and no hassle to meet with teachers after afternoon program. You can even go to the dining hall for a quick snack. There is a good deal of convenience when it comes to being on campus.

On the other hand, Reese ‘26 proposed that though day students might not be as involved at Govs, they get to enjoy a more flexible schedule. “Day students get to go anywhere after their commitments,” she said. Which made me think… day students can escape the chaos that can be right in the center of Govs, including tests, busy schedules, or friend drama. They can spend their time outside of the Byfield bubble, whereas for boarders, it’s more difficult to get off campus.

Another difference between borders and day students is the difference in community. Govs becomes the boarders’ community. They spend most of their time at school during the school year. Whether you are 30 minutes or 15 hours away from home, the connections you make in the dorm and on campus truly mean a lot, with late- night talks, ramen night, and a constant support system. Juliana Lucero ‘26 said, “Coming in as a freshman, the dorm community was one of the greatest support systems I had here.”

However, Reese ‘26 brought up the fact that though you can make a new community at school, you don’t get to interact with your community at home. Some of us have staples in our lives–mainly family–that help us get through day by day. When you are away from those constants, school can be hard. Reese highlighted that going home to her family helps her create stability in her routine.

Besides the challenges and disadvantages, I wouldn’t change one thing. The dorm community has become a second home for me, and I’ll forever cherish these four years. Though I haven’t had the flexibility to go off campus consistently or see my family as often as I wanted, boarding here at Govs has helped me grow as a person and a leader.

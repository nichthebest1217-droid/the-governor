Do you hate coming back from winter break only to get slammed with the midterm exam pressure? Over the past few years, a question has arisen about the winter exam schedule: Should it be moved back to before winter break?

Mid-term exams were originally scheduled before the break. During the 2020–2021 school year, at the height of the COVID-19 pandemic with partially online learning, the school chose not to administer traditional exams. When exams were reinstated in the 2022–2023 school year, the school used that opportunity to reconsider December exams. Mr. Satow said, “One of the big factors was to honor the semester-long classes. It was awkward for them to be giving their semester exam three weeks before the end of the semester.” Most of the semester-long classes are English, History, and Art courses, and giving a “final” exam before the course had actually concluded was inconsistent and unfair to teachers. Moving exams to the true end of the semester allowed those classes to fully complete their content before assessing students.

At the same time, the school shifted from large, subject-based exams in the field house to block-based assessments in the classrooms. Changing this gave teachers more flexibility to use projects, presentations, and alternative formats instead of only long sit-down tests.

When this change was originally made, it was discussed among just the faculty. Student voices were not taken into consideration, although most of us don’t want to take exams in the first place. After sending out a poll, two-thirds of the students who responded want exams to be before the break. When I asked Andrew Melly ‘26 what it’s like coming from a school that had exams before break, he said, “There’s nothing to stress about over break, and you can only relax. Here at Govs, you have the stress of exams hanging over your head while you’re on break.”

Ultimately, when deciding on an exam schedule, not everyone will agree. There is an equal argument from both sides. Having exams after the break may accommodate semester-long courses, but does that make our break less enjoyable and more stressful? When finishing my interview with Mr. Satow, he said, “We’d always be willing to hear people’s opinions. If we’re not listening to new ideas, then we’re not really doing our jobs.” Discussion among our peers allows teachers and students to better understand each other’s perspectives and work toward a schedule that best supports the school community.

It is a recent tradition that Govs’ spring play is student written and directed. However, this year is the first where we have had two plays happening in the same season, both being student written! Marilyn Xie ‘26 and Lucy Shows ‘28 have put in a tremendous amount of work to bring their creative visions to life. They brainstormed scenarios, wrote and revised their scripts, and taught the actors how to speak their lines and move their bodies!

I interviewed Lucy, the writer/director of the play I’m in (which will be performed May 9th and 13th in the Duncan Philips Library). Lucy’s play, *The Ex-Plot*, can be described as a drama-comedy based in a small boarding school where a group of students who hardly know one another must work together after false accusations threaten to get them expelled. What follows are some of her insights into the process and what comes next.

**Q: What was your process of writing this play?**

LS: I wrote this play for English class, designing it around layered characters inspired by people in my own life.

**Q: What did you learn through writing it?**

LS: Writing this play, I learned how difficult it is to write realistic dialogue and decisions. I had to let the plot be driven by the characters rather than vice versa.

**Q: What was the most challenging part of the writing/directing process?**

LS: Directing the play was kind of sprung on me. With more time, I would like to be able to modify the more last-minute comedic elements and have more of a set and technical elements.

**Q: How do you think you’ll do directing a play?**

LS: Directing is a new challenge for me, but I find that as I step into the role, giving notes and thinking about direction for the cast becomes easier and less awkward. It’s difficult to be a younger director than my actors, but it’s a fun challenge!

**Q: If you could tell all of Govs anything to get them excited about the play, what would you say?**

LS: I would say that the play is a mysterious, honest, and sometimes larger-than-life piece centered around the private school experience. Audiences are likely to be familiar with many of the archetypes and should look forward to the unique stage we have in the Duncan Phillips Library.

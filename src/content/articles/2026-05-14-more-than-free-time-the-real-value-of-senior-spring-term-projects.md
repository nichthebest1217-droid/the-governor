Senior spring is the culminating experience of your High School career. Grades really don’t matter anymore; the sun (supposedly) is shining, and you can leave to grab Chipotle midday. Something unique to Govs, however, is the senior spring term project. The program has existed for more than a decade and has allowed seniors to focus on something they are truly passionate about. Did I mention you can drop your least favorite class? Potentially two free blocks??

Come December, Mr DeSalvo sends out an email with the preliminary information about writing your proposal. Then, if needed, you rework your project into something the committee finds difficult enough, yet at the same time, within the realm of reason. Here are a few projects the seniors are doing this year:

- Making Mugs for the Dining Hall
- Building an app
- Filming a documentary
- Creating a cookbook
- Learning how to day trade
- Teaching themselves how to play an instrument

I interviewed a senior who is undergoing the senior spring term process this year, and he thinks that having the projects last the “full second semester would be tough. I’ve heard this sentiment echoed from many of the seniors I’ve talked to. The transition from your regular class can be a little abrupt for both students and teachers. Tests and projects sometimes extend past the deadline or occur the day before dropping, leading to confusion. The student also talked about how the weekly check-ins and progress updates can be restrictive for certain projects that aren’t easy to quantify. A different senior said, “The teachers can sometimes be harsh,” but he understands they have his best interests at heart. “They just want you to have something you’re proud of at the end.”

This isn’t to say that the majority of seniors aren’t fans of the SSTP; in fact, it’s the opposite. Over a third of this year’s senior class has a senior project, big or small. For almost all of them, having something they truly look forward to during the week, while still falling within the right range of difficulty, is a welcome change of pace. This is a credit to the SSTP committee for not allowing an over (or under) ambitious project to slip through the cracks. It also speaks to the care put into shaping each proposal, and while sometimes inconvenient, how the weekly check-ins make sure you don’t lag behind.

The projects culminate in May, specifically in Philips during lunch on Monday, the 18th. Make sure you stop by so you can see what all the effort the seniors have put in amounts to.

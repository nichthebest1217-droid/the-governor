On any given day at Govs, it’s not unusual to see students gathered around a cluster of puppies tumbling over each other in dorms, classrooms, or just on the sidewalk. Behind these moments is Mrs. Borgatti, an AP Environmental Science and Chemistry teacher whose lifelong passion for animals has made her the go-to person on campus for animal care and advice.

“I have loved animals ever since I can remember,” she said. Growing up without pets only strengthened her determination to work with them. That passion carried into college, where she earned her veterinary technician license and began building hands-on experience in animal care.

Mrs. Borgatti’s work alongside veterinarians has shaped both her expertise and her role within the Govs community. After working as a technician in Ohio, she returned to Massachusetts and joined Bulger Animal Hospital, where she gained experience in surgery, anesthesia, and general practice. She balanced this work with teaching, developing a depth of knowledge that students and faculty regularly rely on. Reflecting on the many times she’s been called to help, she joked, “For some reason people think I won’t be nervous, so they call me!”

Her rescues on campus include what was believed to be a bat, but turned out to be a flying squirrel. Other calls involved actual bats in dorms and showers, all of which she safely rescued. In one case, she helped an injured beaver on the side of the road. “Its hip was dislocated and I was able to pop it back in,” she said. The beaver was later rehabilitated and released back into the wild.

Her journey into breeding dogs began unexpectedly. After losing her first dog, her family connected with a breeder of Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retrievers and agreed to foster part of a litter. “After two weeks, she asked us to keep one of the puppies,” Mrs. Borgatti said. That puppy, Lena, had rare genetics that made her an ideal dog for breeding. The Borgatti family is now raising its eighth litter.

For Mrs. Borgatti and her children, the experience is both joyful and meaningful. “It has taught my kids responsibility and how to care for animals,” she said. “Not many kids can spend weeks with that much cuteness and then say goodbye.”

The newest litter, with names inspired by a charcuterie board– Pickles, Manchego, Olive, Chorizo, Fig, and Pita– has been raised right here on campus. Beyond their charm, these dogs often go on to serve important roles as service or search-and-rescue animals. On campus, they provide something equally valuable, like comfort and connection, and help people take their minds off of stressful things like exams and classes.

Even after the puppies leave, their impact on the community remains, thanks to Mrs. Borgatti. Through her dedication, compassion, and energy, she has created something lasting within the Govs community. She is more than a teacher; she is someone who makes campus life more supportive and joyful.

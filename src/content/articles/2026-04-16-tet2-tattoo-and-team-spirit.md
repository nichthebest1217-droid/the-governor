The SMART (Student Modeling A Research Topic) Team at Govs is back at it again! Guided by Dr. Katherine Alex, this year’s winter afternoon program saw a team of nine dedicated members travel to Washington D.C. during spring break to present their findings at the national ASBMB Conference.

This year, we decided to focus on TET2 (Tet methylcytosine dioxygenase 2) – a protein with a name that’s just as tricky to say as it is to study. While not necessarily serving as a direct cause of Parkinson’s Disease (PD), studies have shown that patients with PD often have elevated levels of TET2, demonstrating an interesting correlation. We followed the research trail through scientific journals for months, uncovering TET2’s normal mechanisms and disease pathway, as well as a hopeful potential treatment for PD – SIRT1 (Silence information regulator 1). In simple terms, SIRT1 might help treat PD by lowering both the amount and activity of TET2.

For quite a while, the team seriously (and we mean *seriously*) considered making removable tattoo stickers for distribution at the conference, for the pronunciation of “TET2” sounds very much like “tattoo.” In fact, this coincidence was one of the reasons the protein triumphed during its original competition with other proteins when the team was trying to decide which to research. Other reasons include the fact that PD is a major public health problem that affects as many as one million Americans in any given year, and that TET2 is a critical regulator of DNA methylation, which basically means that it can serve as a light switch that turns the light – in this case, DNA – on and off. Pretty important stuff.

At the conference, we explained all of this and more (minus the tattoo stickers). We had a stunning 3D-printed protein model and a massive science poster created by the team to help illustrate our presentation in great detail.

One of our listeners – an elderly scientist – told the team: “My husband is diagnosed with Parkinson’s, so it is just great to see you young people doing this work.” We were genuinely touched. Her comment was why we were so happy and willing to devote all the long hours of work – not only to grow and learn, but also to contribute to a change that could make a real impact.

Of course, we were also very happy and willing to do our work because every Wednesday, we played board games with Dr. Alex. Every day, the team is immersed in chattering and laughter even at the most random jokes. You’ll find more about these if you join the team for the winter season next year. So why not give it a shot?

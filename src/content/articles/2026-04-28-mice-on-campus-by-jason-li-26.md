If you are a frequent visitor to the Pescosolido Library, you know there is a sign next to the printer, saying “Please DO NOT eat in the library. The food will attract mice, and they will take over the world.” Unfortunately, that has become a reality on campus.

According to many witnesses, mice have been spotted all over the campus. They’ve set foot in the hockey locker rooms as well as the Frost classrooms. The mice have taken over the campus from Eames to Alfond Center, so how should we react to these unexpected visitors?

I interviewed several community members about the mice, including Trent DiGiovanni ‘27. Coming back from an away hockey game, Trent spotted the mouse under someone’s pants in the locker room, and it ran away as soon as spotted. When I asked him if it was dangerous, he laughed and said, “No, I don’t think so.” Trent and his teammates captured the mouse in a box and gave it to Mr. Delay as his early Christmas gift.

I got a similar response from Ms. Fitzgerald. She told me she found a mouse in her classroom one morning. She tried to trap the mouse, but it escaped and hid in the vents. It returned to her classroom that afternoon during class, when she mobilized a troop of students and tried to capture the mouse in their trash can. Ms. Fitzgerald was so excited with her story that I had to interrupt and ask, “Are you not scared of the mice?” She responded, “No, it’s because of the cold weather. Mice are not rats. They are smaller and less dangerous. Rats can give people rabies, but mice usually cannot.”

To understand this situation from a more authoritative perspective, I went to Mr. Woodruff, the head of facilities. He explained to me that his team “responds to approximately 5–10 requests related to rodent issues each month,” and that mice usually come inside due to “building structures and resident habits.” Since mice are small, buildings are never entirely rodent-proof. However, we could do our part by improving our habits. His tips for keeping mice out of the dorms are to throw out trash frequently, close windows, and store food in sealed containers. We can’t change the buildings, but we can for sure change our habits.

Keeping the mice off campus requires the combined effort of everyone. Students can do their part by keeping their rooms clean and storing food properly. And even if you spot a mouse on campus, don’t panic–report it to a faculty member or put a work order online for facilities to take care of it. At this point, though, the mice might as well start paying tuition.

When people hear “boarding school,” they usually picture a campus where everyone lives full-time and the school feels separate from the outside world. Govs isn’t like that. About 60% of students are boarders and 40% are day students, which makes it different from a lot of traditional boarding schools. On top of that, many of the boarders live within two hours of campus. So even though they live at school, students are still pretty connected to home, which changes the overall feel of campus.

I have gone to both Govs and Brewster, a school with 95% boarding and 5% day students, so I have seen the contrast firsthand. At Brewster, everyone stays on campus every weekend due to the fact that no one lives within two hours of campus, and most are a flight away. At Brewster, there is more buy-in from people for campus activities. Everyone attends every Saturday game, every school dance, and every weekend activity because frankly, there is nothing better to do. With this buy-in from the community, the events are more fun and competitive.

At Govs, the dean’s team works hard to put on fun events, dances, and games, but due having many on campus leave Friday through Sunday, this makes the events have less energy. The mix between boarding and day students makes the social life feel more balanced, however. We get to have more of a normal high school experience, whether it is going into Boston for dinner, or watching a local football game. It’s pretty common for a boarder at Govs to get invited to a day student’s house for dinner or even to stay for a weekend. During hockey tournaments or even unexpected events like power outages, boarders often stay with day families or other boarders who live close by. That creates connections that go beyond just campus life.

At Brewster, there were definitely kids from New England, but most students were from all over the country, which made it so cliques didn’t really form as much and people were more mixed together. At the Governor’s Academy, a lot of students, even boarders, are from Massachusetts or nearby, so people sometimes come in already knowing each other. That can lead to more defined friend groups early on.

At the same time, this local presence adds its own kind of diversity, especially in perspectives and backgrounds within the region. Govs feels different because of that mix. The 60/40 split between boarding and day students gives it a unique identity. It still feels like a boarding school, but not in a super isolated way.

Overall, even if Brewster felt tighter as one whole community, Govs creates really strong bonds within smaller friend groups. It ends up being a good balance between independence and having a close, tight-knit group around you.

JV Girls hockey is the only sport where you might see someone fall, laugh, get back up, score a goal, and then celebrate like they just won the Olympics. In the middle of that chaos, though, everyone is trying their absolute hardest. That balance is what defines JV Puck.

The program has been running for 30 years, and at its heart is Coach Karin, now in his 28th season coaching the team. Mr. Karin treats his team with the same seriousness and dedication as any varsity team on campus. He wants the best for his players and for them to all grow. According to Coach Karin, “what sets JV apart is how students continue to play when they have other options that don’t require late-night practices,” something that inspires him every day. That choice–to stay committed even when it isn’t the easiest option–speaks to the kind of culture he and the team has built.

For many players, this is the first time stepping out onto the ice with a stick. Mr. Karin explains, “For most of the girls, playing hockey is new to them. The learning curve can be steep, and players take joy in achieving certain benchmarks, such as stopping, lifting the puck, or backward crossovers.” You will see players who have never skated before practicing alongside those who have grown up playing competitively. Beginners feel safe and supported, and improvement is constant in all players. Every new skill, like a clean stop or a first assist, is noticed and celebrated.

Unlike many varsity teams, who work off of the constant pressure of recruitment and college expectations, JV puck focuses on growth. Lila Sumner ‘28 noted that “the team makes you feel so good about every small achievement, and that kind of positivity is something you carry with you forever.” That positivity shows up everywhere, in cheers from the bench, congratulations in the locker room, and dancing and singing during practice. As Lila explained, “so many varsity teams have a toxic stress that blankets them due to the pressure of recruitment and college, and JV is unburdened with these pressures, which allows us to just have fun and make mistakes to learn.” That freedom creates room for confidence to build and for leadership to develop over time.

Traditions help carry that JV Puck culture from year to year. For example, seniors are given a jersey after four seasons with the team, marking their commitment and growth. The team’s Instagram highlights both the funniest moments and the biggest successes. Most important is the highly anticipated annual JV Girls vs. faculty game which showcases this team’s spirit, mixing competition with humor, while teachers compete against the JV girls players and the varsity players coach.

The legacy of JV Puck isn’t just about goals or wins. It’s about seniors who choose to stay, freshmen who grow into leaders, and a team culture that values effort, humor, support, and love!

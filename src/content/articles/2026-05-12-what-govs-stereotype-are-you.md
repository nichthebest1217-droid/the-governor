If you ever pick up a student handbook (which nobody ever does), you’ll learn official rules, like dress code, graduation requirements, and attendance policies. What you won’t learn are the norms and traditions that make Govs Govs. Whether it’s unspoken rules or school-specific slang, the culture at Govs is shaped by shared norms that students pass on to their friends year after year.

If you spend enough time on campus, you’ll start to notice students falling into a few unofficial categories during PA block. Most people can recognize themselves or their friends in one of the classic campus stereotypes. Take “Stu Reaper” for example. This student seems to live in the Stu during PA block, either playing pool in the back room or just avoiding homework. For any freshman in their first few months, the Stu becomes their natural habitat. Then there’s the “Dhall Rotter,” who turns lunch into a full social event. Long after finishing their food, they stay to talk to their friends. You will also see the “Lib Worm.” Some visit the library to study, while others use it as a quieter place to chat with friends. Finally, there are “Dorm Nappers,” who use lunch and PA as a time to be alone and sleep. These stereotypes capture a funny truth about Govs: if you want to find someone during lunch or PA block, you probably already know exactly where to look.

Another thing that sets Govs apart from other boarding schools is the language we all use on a day-to-day basis. Like any small community, students have developed their own vocabulary and words that make no sense to outsiders. Take a step into any room with students, and you’ll hear “chips” (completely unrelated to food) thrown around a million times in conversation. The mystery of what “chips” even means might be up to interpretation, but it has developed into new terms like “fries” or even “papas,” depending on who you talk to. And then there’s the all too real “Govs Curse.” No matter where you are in the world, you’re never safe from it. These simple phrases shape a unique campus language, only known to Govs.

All students also know there are really two handbooks: the official student handbook and the Govs’ unspoken rulebook. The first thing a new student learns stepping onto the campus is “don’t walk across Sager.” After almost four years at the Academy, it’s rare to witness anyone walking across it. The path to the student center is another example: If you are a real Govs student, you know people never walk on the road; instead, people take the shortcut through the mud. There are many more unspoken rules at Govs, like underclass students are not allowed to sit at the front tables in the Dhall, and seniors wear the “senior backpacks.” These unspoken rules make campus more than just a place; it’s Govs.

Whether you’re a stu reaper or you’re just chips, learning Govs lingo and norms is a rite of passage for all students.

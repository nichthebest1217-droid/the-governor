This year, the Govs community welcomes Mr. Caleb Adams, who has joined the Math department. Mr. Adams teaches Honors Geometry and Honors Algebra 2 and coaches JV Girls Soccer and Varsity Girls Tennis. While he now teaches and coaches at Govs, his career began in a very different setting.

Prior to becoming a teacher, Mr. Adams was a federal consultant working in data analytics and federal credit. His experience with math and data eventually paved the way for his career as an educator, allowing him to bring this unique skill set into the classroom. He later began teaching at Brimmer and May, a day school in Chestnut Hill, MA.

So, what exactly brought him to Govs? For Mr. Adams, the community is a significant part of his interest in Govs: “Govs is such a wonderful community of supportive and collaborative people.” Whether it’s academics and sports or the weekend activities, he says Govs “feels like home.”

He is also surprised by how many areas students excel in and is impressed by how engaged they are in a variety of activities throughout the day.

One of the things Mr. Adams most enjoys teaching is how “versatile math methods are across the disciplines”. Mr. Adams sees math as playing a significant role in fields such as statistics, history, anthropology, biology, engineering, and many more. When teaching math, he says, “I am able to make a meaningful impact and help guide students towards discovering their own passions.” For him, math is not limited to equations and numbers in the classroom; it can be a useful tool across many different areas of education.

Outside of the classroom, Mr. Adams has some interests that students may not expect! He used to volunteer with a dog rescue, and his family has adopted several failed fosters that they decided to keep. He enjoys playing tennis. Besides playing tennis, he also enjoys playing volleyball, card and board games, watching Jeopardy, and traveling.

From math to tennis to traveling, Mr. Adams seems to have mastered many talents, but he is still working on one skill: cooking. As he puts it, “Someday I'll figure out how to be a better cook too.”

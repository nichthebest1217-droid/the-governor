Those of us who are in the student center often have noticed a recent change. Patty MacDonald ran the Grill for many years, serving up sandwiches, burgers, fries, tater tots, and more. Now she is working at Exeter and has been replaced by new staff, but it wasn’t just her food that made her legendary. Patty remembered every student’s name, asked how we were doing, and made a connection with everyone.

Patty was half of the heart of the student center. She and Chris Robinson in the bookstore formed a legendary duo that created a strong connection with the students. Patty always made sure to take a moment to ask about the stresses in the students’ day. Even when lines stretched across the entire room between classes, she was always calm and attentive. Patty had a rare mix of efficiency and intentional care. Walking away from the counter left you with more than just a meal; you left with joy knowing that the food you have been given has been made with love.

The food that Patty made was amazing. My own morning routine was to show up every day to order two bacon, egg, and cheeses; eventually she had them waiting for me the moment I arrived, wrapped in foil to keep the heat in. Each sandwich was top quality crispy bacon, fried eggs, and perfectly melted cheese between toasted plain bagels. Paired with an Arizona iced tea with lemon from Chris at the bookstore, there was no better way to start the day.

Now that Patty has moved on, the difference is obvious. The student center is quieter, emptier. The loud, crowded energy is gone, and the new grill workers have big shoes to fill. Seniors especially lost that daily constant of seeing Patty and starting their day with that familiar spark. Students still talk about her and miss the impact she had. It wasn’t just food it was the connection, the routine, the way she brought smiles to everyone.

Change is not always easy. This isn’t the same Govs it was five years ago. The grill may not feel the same right now and that’s okay. Now it’s up to the new workers and the students to make new memories and new routines. Over time, the student center can feel alive again in a new way.

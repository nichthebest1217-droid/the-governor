Many students may think that teachers do not live interesting lives during summer break. However, teachers have their own hobbies, trips, and other jobs, and they spend time preparing for the next school year. Mr. Ogden and Mrs. Borgatti both spent their summer breaks both traveling and preparing for the next school year.

Mr. Ogden traveled to Korea and China during the summer, where he met some of his former students and their families, giving him a chance to reconnect with students he had taught in the past. The trip allowed him to experience a whole new part of the world while making strong connections through their shared love of golf.

He also played golf and participated in a golf tournament with his brother. This was especially meaningful for him because Mr. Ogden’s brother had been dealing with a health problem and had undergone a bilateral lung transplant. For Mr. Ogden, winning a tournament mattered less than simply being able to spend time with his brother.

Mr. Ogden said, “I have won tournaments before, and that's always fun. But the real victory, the meaningful win -- being able to spend time together. Lots of players came by after the tournament to congratulate us, and what they all said or suggested was that just being there and being together was a win! And they were happy to be a part of it.”

Mrs. Borgatti also spent a lot of her summer doing outdoor activities. She led people on trail rides in Vermont and also spent time mountain biking. The highlight of her summer came when she traveled to Alaska and stayed on the Kenai Peninsula for about ten days. She went fishing and horseback riding, and also came face-to-face with a grizzly bear. She caught fish during the trip and sent some of the fresh fish home.

These summer activities connected to what she teaches at Govs. As an Environmental Science teacher, she looks for things in nature that she can later use in her class. For example, she took pictures of sights during her trips to use when she teaches. For her, preparation for her classes does not get a vacation! Ms. Borgatti said that she also worked as a consultant outside of Govs in Vermont. As she suggested, many Govs teachers work jobs and continue to learn during their summers.

Mr. Ogden and Mrs. Borgatti show that summer break is not only a time when teachers take a break from school. It is also a time when teachers can recharge, have new experiences, and prepare themselves before returning to school.

2026 Spring Issue # 4 Editorial by Adam Krogmeier ’27, Tyler Passero ’26, Alexandra (Alex) Hughey ’27, Rhoads Kent ’27

Over four years at Govs, weekend events have had a pretty underwhelming reputation, with many either having little to no guests show up or mainly attracting underclassmen. But why is that? Many different faculty members whose jobs are to plan weekend events have come and gone, so each year has been different from the last. 

The off-campus trips, to places like the Topsfield Fair or Boston, have remained a constant over the past three years. The on-campus events, however, have changed a lot. There used to be more on-campus games like capture the flag and inflatable bouncy castles. But these events seem to have disappeared over the past two years. The students have noticed, and it has taken away from the overall sense of community.

Many day students often don’t return to campus on the weekend because there isn’t a strong enough reason to come back. For day students, coming back to campus means giving up time with family and friends, so the event has to feel worth it. Events don’t feel hyped enough, and the community doesn’t promote them to make that extra trip feel worthwhile. It becomes a cycle: when fewer day students attend, the energy drops, making the entire event less appealing to everyone. When weekend events lack energy, staying off campus becomes the more appealing and easier choice. This raises an ongoing question: how can the school break this cycle and bring more day students back to campus on the weekends?

Behind the scenes, a group of students recently took on an important role in shaping weekend life at Govs. Tasked with interviewing prospective deans whose biggest focus would be on weekend life, students became active participants in deciding who would best fit the role. We asked thoughtful questions focused on how candidates would create more engaging weekends for boarding students while also encouraging day students to return to campus. By directly interacting with the prospective deans, students helped ensure that future weekends will better reflect the interests and needs of the entire community. That kind of student involvement is especially important given the challenges weekend life currently faces.

A solution to getting students to attend school events could be making weekend events at the Academy mandatory for both boarding and day students. Although some students might find this frustrating, or a “waste of time,” it will ultimately strengthen our community. When more students attend, events tend to feel more energetic, engaging, and enjoyable, which can lead to new friendships and a more connected student body. 

We should be excited to do activities together with our friends, not trying to avoid them. In the end, increasing participation in weekend activities is key to creating a more fun and exciting weekend experience for everyone.

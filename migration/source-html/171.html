by Toby Grenier ‘26

Senior pranks have long been a highlight of the spring semester at The Governor’s Academy, combining humor, creativity, and a sense of community. From harmless jokes to elaborate plans, seniors use this tradition to celebrate the close of their time on campus. As Ms. Fitzgerald, a longtime faculty member, explains, “I have been here for 40 years, and there have always been pranks.”

Over the years, some pranks have become especially memorable, standing out for their creativity or humor. Ms. Fitzgerald recalls examples such as hundreds of carefully placed cups covering the entire lobby of Wilkie, forcing anyone who entered to navigate the space slowly and carefully to avoid spilling them. She also remembers students saran wrapping faculty cars, turning an ordinary parking lot into a scene of confusion and laughter. These pranks exemplify the creativity and humor that define the tradition. The most successful pranks are the ones that surprise the community while remaining harmless. 

While the tradition of pranks has persisted, the approach has altered. According to Ms. Fitzgerald, “seniors as a whole have become more responsible and less destructive in planning their pranks.” However, she also noted that this can sometimes lead to a lack of creativity, resulting in the repetition of the same prank throughout multiple senior classes. In recent years, for example, many seniors have relied on the same prank of moving backpacks during morning meetings, confusing underclassmen but ultimately creating little lasting impact. This repetition suggests that modern pranks do not always match the originality of earlier efforts.

The administration’s response to senior pranks plays a key role in shaping the tradition. Mrs. Fitzgerald alluded to the fact that the school is “usually okay as long as the academic day is not interrupted, the pranks aren’t dangerous or destructive, and it doesn’t require the maintenance team to clean up.” The guidelines create clear boundaries for the students to follow, drawing the line between a successful prank and one that goes too far. She emphasizes that, “the seniors need to be responsible and fix whatever has been disturbed,” encouraging students to think carefully about how their actions affect others and themselves. 

As this year’s seniors approach graduation, anticipation is already building around what their prank might be. With such a long standing tradition behind them, the pressure is on to create something that is both original and respectful of the school’s expectations. Whichever path they take, it will undoubtedly reflect their legacy as a class and add another chapter to the long story of senior pranks at The Governors Academy.

by Trent DiGiovanni ‘27

When Mr. C arrived at The Governor’s Academy in 1990, he came with a simple goal: to teach and coach high school students. Thirty-five years later, as he prepares to retire, he leaves behind a legacy that has shaped generations of Govs students both in and out of the classroom.

Before Govs, Mr. C taught at the Fay School, a junior boarding school in Southborough. Though he valued his experience there, he wanted to coach and work with older, more mature kids. Through a colleague from the Fay School, he found his way to Govs in 1990 to fulfill his dream. 

When he arrived at Govs, his coaching career began with the Varsity Boys Hockey team, where he coached for nine years, seven of those as head coach. He then transitioned to coaching the Varsity Girls hockey team. When I asked him what the transition was like, he said, “In some ways, it was a blessing in disguise. It was different than coaching the guys, but for me it was better.” 

Among his years of coaching, one season that stood out to him was 2007-2008, Alex Carpenter’s freshman year. That year, they had seven forwards and three defenders, about half of the normal size of a hockey team. The short bench didn’t stop them. The team reached the New England Tournament championship game, which went into double overtime. The other team received a penalty, and Govs scored the game-winning goal. Mr. C said, “That memory, that group of kids, that year, and what we did with the size of the team, that was a special group. Some of them were seniors, but Alex and two kids that she played with were in their freshman years, so I got the chance to work with them for three more years.” 

While at Govs, Mr. C didn’t just coach hockey; he has remained in the History Department throughout his tenure; he has also served as senior class advisor, dorm head in Eames for 29 years, and weekend program coordinator. He coached football during his first nine years and has coached golf since 1997. 

Yet when asked about the most rewarding part of his career, he reflects on the relationships he has formed while living in the dorm, being a senior class advisor, mentoring students, and fully immersing himself in campus life. His own three sons grew up at Governor’s, making the school not just a workplace, but home. 

In retirement, Mr. C looks forward to spending time with family, especially as a seventh grandchild arrives this summer. He plans to spend time at his family’s home in New Hampshire, enjoying simple moments outdoors with his children and grandchildren.

As he steps away from Governor’s, the community thanks Mr. C for his dedication, mentorship, and steady presence. His impact will continue to be felt long after his final class and final practice. So when you see Mr. C walking around campus, thank him for all he has done for this community.

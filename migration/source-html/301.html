by Jayden Padilla ’26

Every year, the seniors at The Governor’s Academy take a trip to Fenway Park to watch a Red Sox game. It’s a big tradition but last year the energy in the Govs section was different; they weren’t just there to see the worst team in the MLB, the Boston Red Sox. They were there to see one of their own in the visitor’s bullpen in a Chicago White Sox uniform: Shane Smith ‘17. In the heat of the warmups Smith took a moment to toss a baseball to a Govs student, a small gesture that shows the character of the man who has become a campus legend.

Before he was an MLB All star, Shane Smith was a powerhouse at Govs. He was a three sport varsity captain in football, basketball, and baseball. According to his former baseball coach, Mr. Kinnealey, Shane’s success was never just about raw talent. It was about a blue collar work ethic that set the tone for everyone around him. “Shane was an eye contact, handshake, hard work guy,” Mr Kinnealey recalls. “He was the kid who would carry the equipment bag as a senior.” 

Mr Kinnealey shared a story that shows Smith’s competitive spirit. During a game against St Sebastian’s, the opposing dugout was “chirping” at Smith while he was on the mound. Most players would lose their cool, but Smith just looked over at the Govs dugout and smiled at his coaches. He let his arm do the talking, shutting them down and proving that he thrived under pressure. It’s the “quiet confidence” that Mr Kinnealey believes allowed Smith to navigate the grind of the minor leagues after going undrafted and recovering from Tommy John Surgery.

One of the most inspiring parts of Smith’s journey is how he gave back to Govs during his rise to the pros. While he was playing in Triple A, Smith made a return to campus to help coach our Thirds Basketball team. I was on that team and I saw firsthand that he didn’t treat it like a hobby. He pushed us to be better athletes. He showed us how to condition properly and gave us a great work ethic. As a result of that we had the best season thirds basketball has ever had with six wins and seven losses.

Smith returned earlier this year to speak to the student body, and a lot of the athletes were eager to hear from the guy who sat in the same classrooms they do now. Now, when I turn on my xbox to play MLB The Show 26 and throw a no hitter against the future academy president Janciel Martinez using Smith’s 95 MPH heater, I find myself yelling “that’s my coach!” Shane Smith is more than just a pitcher; he is proof that hard work and humility can take you wherever you want to go.

by Sophie Kelly ’26

One of the biggest complaints you’ll hear on campus at Govs is that reach is annoying or that the leave policy is overbearing. For most students, the idea of being able to leave campus with their friends to grab a coffee or a quick snack is unrealistic. Because Govs is a closed campus, students are stuck here from Monday to Friday almost every week, until senior spring. 

Every spring, seniors are allowed to come and go from campus during their free blocks. After three and a half years of being trapped on campus, students are finally able to experience the freedom of an open campus. So, how much do seniors really utilize this month of freedom before graduation? 

To find out more, I sent a survey to the senior class. The results showed that 85.7% of seniors plan on taking advantage of these newly gained privileges. Only a slim 14.3% of people said they did not plan to use their senior leave privileges. 

For those who did plan on leaving campus during their free blocks or PA, there was variation in how often. 43% of seniors said they plan on using leave privileges every day. 38% said they planned on leaving campus 3 to 4 times a week. The remaining 17% of students said they only planned to leave 1 to 2 times per week. The majority of students who said they planned on using senior leaves plan on leaving quite often. This is most likely a result of such a strict policy during the first three and a half years as students at Govs. After living on a closed campus, when students are finally given senior privileges, they are more inclined to leave rather than stay. 

As seniors make up for their lost freedoms in previous years, they visit several popular destinations in the greater Newburyport area. The top three destinations include Starbucks, Aroma Joe’s, and downtown Newburyport as a whole. Other popular places included Chipotle, Plum Island, Dunkin, or even home.  Even if it’s just a short trip to grab a coffee, senior leaves mean that seniors spend less time on campus interacting with the rest of the Govs community. For better food or a change of scenery, as seniors utilize their leave privileges, their presence on campus decreases. This subtle shift marks the beginning of underclassmen stepping up and the end of four years for graduating seniors. 

Ultimately, senior leave privileges do more than just give students new freedoms. They help transition underclassmen into the next school year and reveal how four years of restricted leave policies shape the community.

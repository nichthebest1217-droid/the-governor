by Adam Krogmeier ‘27

In the past three years, the baseball team at The Governor’s Academy has seen an unusual turnover of coaches, with three different head coaches in just three years. Change can sometimes be a good thing by shifting movement and goals, but it can also be difficult for players who are just trying to develop fundamentals and succeed together. Every coach has a different approach to the game. One may be all about discipline and structure, while another may prioritize freedom and creativity. While both philosophies can be successful, it is difficult to have to switch back and forth between them.

For the 2024 season the coach was James Cramphin, whose coaching style consisted of well disciplined fundamentals and strong habits that created consistent results.  He was replaced in 2025 by James Baker, who focused primarily on pitching fundamentals and breaking down every little piece of movement. The 2026 season brings Carson Cross. He was drafted in 2015 by the St. Louis Cardinals and played for UNH for four years before that as a stand out pitcher. His coaching style has not yet been displayed but the team is eager to hit the field with him in Florida to create the bond Govs baseball has always been about. 

Another aspect of the game that has been impacted is team chemistry. It takes time for a good baseball team to build a level of trust between the players and the coaches and learn each other’s habits and tendencies. When a coach resigns after only one year, all of that trust goes out the window for the falling year. The players then have to work to build that trust with a new coach. For younger players, it can be difficult to know what to expect from year to year and who will be leading the team. 

However, this has forced the players to build resilience and mental toughness. We have had to adapt quickly, communicate more effectively, and remain focused on the game under uncertain conditions. The captains and upperclassmen have had to take on an even bigger role to keep the team together during this time of transition. In a way, the players–not the coaches– have become the backbone of the program. This year, the veteran players must work to build the team chemistry and to carry forward the best practices of past coaches. 

Looking ahead, many of the athletes hope for greater consistency so that the team can develop and establish an identity. Consistency in coaching would enable the players to learn and develop over several years of a particular coaching system, which would help establish a sense of tradition.

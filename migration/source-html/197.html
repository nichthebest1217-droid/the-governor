by Vincent Liu ‘27

Have you ever wondered what it feels like to be called “Dr.” instead of “Mr.” or “Ms.”? Dr. Alex, the AP Biology teacher at Govs, received her PhD from Case Western Reserve University. She focused on neuroscience and neuropharmacology, specifically on the interaction between serotonin and dopamine, and used laboratory rats for professional experimental procedures. I interviewed Dr. Alex and asked about her opinion on whether today’s students should pursue a PhD. As the old saying goes, “All that glitters is not gold.”

VL: What was the most challenging part of your PhD experience?

KA: In the labs I did in my high school and college, students always knew what reactions they would get, and teachers made sure the lab was going to work. That gave me a false impression of what real science was. When I tried to do a PhD, I almost never got the expected result. 99 out of 100 experiments didn’t show anything at all. During my PhD, we kept a lab journal recording the number of each rat we used in the experiment. I know that the number got up to like 405.

VL: How does your PhD experience influence you as a teacher?

KA: A lot of high school teachers tell you exactly the method to use, the exact hypothesis to test, and what you expect to get. I try to have students design their own procedure and design their own hypothesis, and then evaluate their hypothesis. It doesn’t matter if you got good data or not. It matters whether you can take the data that you’ve got and interpret what it means and what you would do differently next time. 

It’s like making a cake. You figure out how to make a cake, like researching what is going to react together to make the cake rise. You may end up getting a cake or getting a cake soup, but I don’t care. I care what you could do differently next time to get closer to that cake. In real science, you never get a cake the first time.

VL: How could students know if pursuing a PhD is the right decision to make?

KA: Students who might enjoy doing a PhD usually feel amazed when learning something new and appreciate the complexities of the subject. But a PhD is long and arduous, and it could be a really bad choice if your heart isn’t in it. My recommendation to students is always to try to get some research experience in a lab, in college, or in the summer, even if it’s an unpaid internship, so that you know whether it’s something you like or not. 

Is your heart tilting toward earning a PhD? Dr. Alex mentions that earning a PhD requires curiosity and persistence, but it is also rewarding and offers many job opportunities in pharmaceuticals and private sector industries. A PhD might not be for everyone, but it will never hurt to learn more about the actual experience of it!

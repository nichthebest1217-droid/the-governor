2026 Spring Issue #1 Editorial by Isabella Gomez ‘26, Amber Ding ‘26, Toby Grenier ‘26

Community is the heartbeat of Govs. An outsider would think that programming keeps this school going, assuming that the fun hockey games or themed lunches keep the spirit alive, but it’s the niche moments that members of the community provide that fill our cups when they are empty. It’s our teachers who stay late after class to help us with that stingy math problem or our teammates who cheer us on after we make mistakes. When we support and lean on each other during difficult times, we can find pockets of growth and love.

Sometimes, the fast pace of Govs can mask these support outlets. It is easy to get caught up in insignificant things, but if we look outwards we can appreciate the real core of what makes Govs the school that it is. What are the true reasons why students are so comfortable and passionate about their involvement on campus?

The answer reveals itself every week on our campus. Take Friday, 2/13, for example. In the afternoon, ceramics students sold handmade pottery to support the First Parish Newbury Food Pantry, and the Valentine’s flower sale filled the dining hall with many smiles. That evening, girl’s basketball played a spirited game against Pingree, and the cast, pit band, and tech crew of the musical Mamma Mia! made the stage come alive. On 2/17, students celebrating the Chinese New Year dined together in Boston. During 2/19 – 2/22, Harvard Model Congress delegates simulated the U.S. Government with their knowledge and wit.

In such diversity, community blooms. From stage lights in Bergman Theater to goals scored on the basketball court, from intercultural celebrations to academic pursuits, each community member’s contribution is valued, and their talents and perspectives help the campus remain deeply connected and vibrant.

Even beyond campus, this talent still thrives. Govs continues to celebrate alumna Alex Carpenter ‘11 as she represents the country at the 2026 Winter Olympics. Long before she represented the USA on the world’s biggest stage, Carpenter wore cardinal threads and the Governor’s crest. Carpenter’s career at Govs was nothing short of dominant. In just 100 games, she recorded an astonishing 427 points (239 goals and 181 assists), etching herself into the Govs history books as one of the most prolific players in school history. Her scoring ability, vision, and competitive drive elevated the girls’ hockey program and set a new standard for excellence. Carpenter continued her career at Boston College, where she quickly became one of the top players in NCAA women’s hockey and won the Patty Kazmaier Award, given annually to the best player in women’s Division I hockey.

Carpenter has made us proud by bringing the Byfield spirit to the larger world. For current Governor’s student athletes, Carpenter’s journey serves as proof that dedication at Govs can lead anywhere, even to Olympic glory.

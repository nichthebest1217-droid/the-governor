by Maxwell Donahue ‘26

As we return to campus, for the seniors, more than just the temperature has changed. Only two short months until graduation, and the long-awaited “senior privileges” have arrived. But what exactly do those privileges entail? Going out to lunch midday? Senior sliding? Leaving last from morning meeting? The latter is especially true, but the rule that is the most impactful in the coming weeks isn’t a privilege at all.

As Ms. Davey will undoubtedly announce upon returning from March break, all seniors are placed under a pseudo-censure. In the words of the student handbook, this means that “even if it is the senior’s first offense, the senior may be required to appear before the Discipline Committee and may face dismissal, regardless of the student’s prior record.” In talking with Ms. Fitzgerald, ex-dean of students, about why this rule is in place, she said that it’s to prevent seniors without a previous major school rule violation under their belts from thinking they can do anything once and get away with it.

But is automatic censure a good thing? Does this rule really prevent students from getting out of line? I would argue no. I interviewed seniors from other prep schools to see if they had any system like this. One responded, “What? Is that a real thing?” Even without senior spring censure, these other schools still manage without mass outbreaks of delinquency. 

At a time when seniors are supposed to be preparing for complete independence and the college experience, the threat of one mistake potentially ruining your future is problematic. Senior privileges should reflect the trust we’ve built over these four years. Increased levels of freedom would, in my opinion, make students less likely to rebel because they feel appreciative of the trust being placed in them and wouldn’t want to break it.

I’m not saying that consequences shouldn’t exist; in fact that’s integral to growth. However, this pseudo-censure operates on the notion that if left unchecked, seniors will push the rules until the breaking point. I would argue that, if anything, senior spring is when students are most aware of what they have to lose. After four years of work, all culminating in getting a diploma, I think very few would be willing to jeopardize that, censure or no. I propose getting rid of the censure policy because if we are ready to be fully independent adults in August, we deserve the benefit of the doubt in March.

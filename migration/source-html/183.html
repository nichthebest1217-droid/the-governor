by Rhoads Kent ‘27

It’s 10:55 AM on a Friday morning. You just had your first two classes of the day, and your next class does not start until 2:15 PM. As you part ways with your boarder friend heading back to their dorm for a three-hour nap, you consider going to the crowded Dining Hall to yell across the table so your friends can hear you, or going to the Student Center to “rot.”

One option that is not available is leaving campus during this time because the Governors Academy has strict rules regarding day student driving privileges. Many day students want more freedom to balance work and happiness. With an open campus, some might be able to go home, while others could just go to Newburyport. Governor’s should reconsider the driving policy to allow day students to be able to leave campus during the school day.

As stated in the handbook, day students are not allowed to leave campus during the school day unless they have special permission from the Deans. Day students are also not allowed to drive boarders around to different locations on campus. I interviewed English teacher Mr. Robertson, who said that it has been a closed campus since 2001. When asked why he thought day students are limited to using their cars only for travel to and from home, he said, “The purpose is to limit traffic on campus because with little kids and pets, it is a safety concern.” 

He also mentioned a case around 2012 when some seniors broke the rules and drove to a quarry in Gloucester. One girl broke her back jumping into the water. Mr. Robertson said  “students took advantage of their freedom in that situation and made a bad choice.”

When discussing possible changes to the policy, Mr. Robertson said, “I think there would need to be attention to accountability. I don’t think they want students picking up and leaving without making sure that they check in with Reach.” I asked Mr. Robertson if there is anything about these rules that you think students don’t fully see from the school’s perspective. He responded, “I think students may not realize how responsible we as a boarding school feel for the safety of the students.”

Although the day student driving policy restrictions can feel limiting to day students, it is clear that the policy is shaped by the school’s valid safety concerns. Students often see the issue through the lens of fairness and independence, while faculty prioritize responsibility and risk. Perhaps a new policy could be created that would accommodate all of these priorities, giving teachers peace of mind and day students a break from campus.

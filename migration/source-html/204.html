by Maxwell Donahue ‘26

Many Govs Students, myself included, take our current schedule for granted. The 8:15 starts, the intermittent Thursday sleep-ins, and at most, four classes a day. Many of us don’t know or don’t think about how the schedule has evolved. According to an alumnus of the pre-COVID days, the schedule “was hard to keep track of.” What we now consider an early start was a sleep in, and the idea of popping into Dhall to grab a snack between classes was unheard of.

Before COVID and online/hybrid classes, students attended five classes each day (except Wednesday), each lasting 50 minutes with a five-minute passing period in between. This meant constant shuffling between buildings, and if you arrived even a minute late, class was likely to already be in full swing. This older schedule also lacked the Thursday morning convocation block, which was originally on Tuesday afternoons, depriving students of the occasional much-needed recharge. 

As a person who has lived both schedules in his time as a student and teacher, Sr. Norton offers a unique view into the subject. As a student, he would have rather had the current schedule, but when asked if as a teacher he thought it would be helpful to meet four times a week, he said, “I do,” and that having shorter classes could be a good thing: “It keeps your guys attention better. Sometimes for an hour you have to drag it a little bit.” 

The change in schedule was, according to Ms. Block, the chair of the scheduling committee, “In response to COVID.” During quarantine, teachers saw how exhausted students were becoming, and this made them more vulnerable to infection. In our interview Ms Block told me that they wanted to prioritize sleep and restoration, so the 7:45 start time on Tuesdays, Wednesdays, and Fridays was first to go. Next, since everyone had felt so disconnected during purely online classes, they needed to reintegrate morning meetings and convocations so the school could come together again. The result led us to the schedule we have now, but it wasn’t meant to last forever.

After quarantine and the threat of COVID had largely passed, the committee sent out a survey and looked at the schedules for other prep schools around us. In the end, the topics Ms. Block told me they deemed most important were: “consistent start and stop times, an 8:15 start, and 10-minute passing time between classes.” This change to the travel time coincided with the introduction of the Alfond Center. As anyone who’s ever had to make the trek knows, five minutes would make it impossible to get to class on time.

A committee has been formed to consider a new schedule for the future. Once again, we will have to face the difficult questions: How often should classes meet? How long should classes be? And, most importantly, how can we make the school better?

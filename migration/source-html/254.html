by Brodie Donovan ‘26

If you have ever complained about the dorm, wished the gym wasn’t old, or sat in a class and thought it belonged in the stone age, I have some good news for you regarding the future of The Governor’s Academy. The Academy’s strategic plan lays out a vision for the future of the campus and includes some major changes. According to Paul Nardone, Head of the Board of Trustees, the plan is to build three brand new buildings: an athletic and wellness facility, a new dorm, and a new academic building. This plan seeks to update many of the facilities on Govs campus while also preserving the older feel. 

The athletic and wellness facility is in the most exciting news. Currently, the Alumni Memorial Gymnasium consists of an: exercise room with limited weight racks, few machines, and a small size that easily becomes overcrowded; a field house with three basketball courts surrounded by an indoor track that isn’t fit to hold meets; and many outdated locker rooms. A more modern facility would be a game changer not just for varsity athletes but the whole Govs community as a whole. 

The dormitories fit the look of the oldest boarding school in America, but with age comes outdated living facilities. Living in Ingham Dormitory, I can say there’s definitely a lot of interior and exterior work needed. For everyone who lives on campus, the dorm is considered our home and has the power to affect our daily lives. From newer bathrooms to improved common rooms where students can bond together, the addition of modern dorms can change the living experience entirely for boarders. 

Then there is the new academic building. When I sit in a classroom in Frost, I feel a sense of pride but also isolation from the present day. The Board of Trustees plans to combine a traditional feel with a more up to date approach to classroom design. A modern classroom could change the way students learn and teachers teach. A lot of other prep schools we compete with have these newer learning environments, so this feels like a much needed change. 

Nardone made it clear that the Board of Trustees is committed to upgrading Govs campus while also maintaining the history that comes along with it. He says, “Our Board of Trustees remains focused on continuing to invest in maintaining and upgrading our campus infrastructure over the next five years.” This matters because there are many older buildings that need some love and should not be neglected with newer buildings being built. The bottom line is that the strategic plan is a pretty big deal. Most of us won’t be at Govs to see it come to life, but for future students this change could shape what it means to go to Govs.

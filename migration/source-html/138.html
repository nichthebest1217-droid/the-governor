by Rhoads Kent ‘27

Every spring, students enter one of the most challenging and chaotic times of the year…course selection. What seems like a simple “pick your classes” situation quickly turns into a stressful experience that feels like doing a thousand-piece puzzle. The biggest issue, though, is that no one knows what is going on. These are some of the biggest challenges for students who are navigating the course selection process:

1. Course Selection Convocation: There are many problems with this. It happens at 8:15 on a Thursday morning, when no one is awake and able to think about their future. Then all the department heads go up and project a slide that organizes the classes that no one understands. By the time it’s over, students leave feeling overwhelmed, overtired, and even more confused than they were before. 

2. The appeal process: Students scroll through online documents with vague descriptions and extraneous prerequisites. They stumble upon big words like ‘Department Placements’ and ‘appeal,’ which might as well mean ‘good luck.’ At some point, it starts feeling less like course selection and more like a test you didn’t study for. 

3. Your advisor has no idea what is actually going on: You meet with them and leave feeling confident in your classes, only to question every decision you have made since 6th grade the next minute. Sure…your advisor might, if you’re lucky, bring you donuts, but can they tell you about computer science or robotics and give you a suggestion that will actually work? The short answer is…no?

4. The scheduling conflict: You think you have just found the perfect class. It’s something you’re interested in, the teacher is not too hard, and it looks good for college. Then you realize it is actually a semester-long class that doesn’t fit your schedule. Cool.

5. No APs: To top it all off, there is the big question: What will APs look like next year? Administrators dance around the word ‘AP,’ phasing in ‘Advanced’ instead, and no one knows what that might mean or when that might happen. For years, AP exams have been long seen as the highest academic honor—challenging and slightly terrifying. With the school now trying to move away from AP classes, students are left with questions about what will replace them and if those classes will carry the same weight.

In the end, the course selection process is about making the best decisions for your future self with the (little) information that you have. Maybe the lesson here is not to build the perfect schedule, but to learn to navigate difficult decisions and accept that sometimes you might have to just throw in the towel and “trust” the process because somehow, it always ends up okay.
